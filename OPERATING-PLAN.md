# The Engine — the recurring weekly plan

Not a project with an end. An **engine that repeats every week** — and each turn
makes the next one easier (more makers, more content, more audience, more proof,
better matching). You talk to people, you make videos, you tune the product, you
learn. Forever, until it's working — then you scale it.

## The weekly loop (the repeating unit)
Five moves, every week:

1. **GO — field.** Go out: an event, a market, a neighborhood. Talk to makers +
   one organizer. Onboard them on the spot (the card kit). Film everything.
   → grows supply + footage + relationships + learning.

2. **SHOW — content.** Cut **1 long-form maker story** + **daily shorts** from
   the footage. Post consistently.
   → grows audience + brand.

3. **TUNE — product.** Improve the matching / re-ranker + kill friction, based on
   the **real makers you just onboarded** (not in a vacuum).
   → the product gets better on real data.

4. **MOVE — funding.** Push **one** BID / organizer relationship one step forward.
   → toward the first paycheck.

5. **LEARN — review (~1 hr).** What worked? What flopped? What's the data saying?
   Adjust the strategy.
   → the engine gets smarter each turn.

Repeat. That's the whole thing.

## What you're testing (the bet, made explicit)
Every cycle is an experiment, not blind execution. You're testing:
- **Do makers actually engage and stick** — or do they ghost after onboarding?
- **Does the matching/convening actually help them** — do real collabs/events
  happen? *(This is the core magic, and the real unproven thing. Watch it hardest.)*
- **Does the content resonate and grow** — which stories/angles land?
- **Will an organizer/BID pay** — the money hypothesis.

You steer based on what the data says. The plan is a **learning loop**, not a script.

## The signals to watch (so you know it's working)
Weekly: makers onboarded · how many actually got value · connections/collabs
formed · events happening · content views + follower growth · BID/organizer
conversations advancing.

**The one that matters most: are makers actually getting helped?** (found, booked,
connected, collaborating.) If yes, everything follows. If no, that's the fix.

## How it evolves (first ~90 days — engine same, focus shifts)
- **Weeks 1–4:** mostly GO + SHOW. Onboard your first ~20–40 makers in **one**
  neighborhood. Learn how onboarding really lands. Start posting.
- **Weeks 4–8:** the makers you onboarded start connecting → spark the **first
  collab/event** (real proof). Audience building. First organizer/BID conversation.
- **Weeks 8–12:** the **first real event happens** (proof + content + case study).
  Take the BID pilot meeting with proof in hand. Re-ranker tuned on real data.

By ~90 days: a body of real makers, a content catalog + growing audience, one
real event, real product data, and a warm BID pilot. That's the engine's first
full turn.

## The discipline (so you don't burn out)
- **Sustainable rhythm.** You're solo and stretched. Something like 2 field days,
  1–2 make days, light tune/move/learn. Bend it to your life. **Consistency beats
  intensity** — the engine only works if it keeps turning.
- **Every action measured against Maya** (the litmus test in THE-HEART.md).
- **Guard the two failure modes:** (a) all building, no people; (b) all content,
  no build/proof. The five-move loop forces balance — don't skip GO or MOVE.

## The mindset
You're not "launching." You're **running a flywheel and learning out loud.** Each
week: more makers helped, more stories told, a smarter product, a warmer payday,
a sharper strategy. Slow at first, then it compounds. The job is just to **keep
the engine turning** — and to watch honestly whether makers are actually getting
helped, because that's the truth everything else rests on.
