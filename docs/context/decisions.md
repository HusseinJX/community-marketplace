## 2026-08-13 — maps, directions, saved businesses, event filters

Full write-up in CHANGELOG.md. The decisions worth keeping:

- **A basemap is context, not content.** Seven maps, one `BaseTiles`, on `mapbox/light-v11` —
  streets-v12 puts coloured roads, parks and a POI label on every corner in front of the pins the
  map exists to show. Two systems had drifted because five maps were hardcoded to OSM while two
  read a token that was already set.
- **Coordinates beat an address, and a real fix beats a centroid.** Directions send the pin, not
  the typed address a geocoder has to guess at again. The events map was placing everything on one
  of seven neighbourhood centroids on the stated grounds that vendor_events had no lat/lng — untrue
  since `20260711120000`. Same rule the proximity work set: never print a district centre as a
  thing's location.
- **The href is the Google URL, always; iPhones intercept the click.** Deciding it by sniffing the
  device during render is a hydration mismatch, and deciding it in an effect leaves the button
  briefly dead. `target="_blank"` is mandatory — the iOS shell allows `*.apple.com` navigation for
  Sign in with Apple, so a same-tab maps.apple.com link renders Apple's map web page *inside the
  app*.
- **A `<button>`, not an `<a>`, inside a card.** The cards are one big Link and an anchor inside an
  anchor is silently un-nested by the browser, breaking both.
- **A control that pretends is worse than no control.** The profile's Save was local `useState` —
  saved until you reloaded. Two fake buttons would have been the "consistent" answer; the real one
  was to build `saved_members` as an exact sibling of `saved_events` and point both at it.
- **Its own table, not a polymorphic "saved things".** An event id and a member id come from
  different systems and nothing reads them together; a `kind` column buys one table and costs a
  filter on every query. `member_id` is TEXT for the same reason `event_id` is — connector ids like
  `pliq_361` sit beside uuids, and a uuid column makes most of the directory unsaveable.
- **A filter row must survive returning no results.** Hiding the controls along with the results
  leaves no way back, so the row stays and says "nothing matches".
- **Clear belongs at the HEAD of a scrolling filter row**, not the end — at the end it sits off the
  side of the screen, behind the very pills you are trying to undo.
- **Don't read the clock during render.** Map pins skip the "today" pulse rather than take an
  impure timestamp that the server and browser can disagree about.
- **One container for the whole page.** Nav, banner, search and cards were at three different left
  edges; they now share `max-w-6xl px-4 md:px-8`.
- **A sticky nav must outrank the promo banner.** The banner was `z-40` under a `z-30` header and
  scrolled over it. Fixed by lowering the banner to `z-20`, not by raising the header — 19 modals
  live at `z-50`.
- **A tab's width is part of its identity.** Shops, Products and all three Events views now share
  one container; the dated list becomes a two-column grid on desktop rather than stretching, so
  switching views never moves the page's edges.

## 2026-08-13 — home tabs, curated listings, posting is vendors-only

Full write-up in CHANGELOG.md under the 2026-08-13 heading. The decisions worth keeping:

- **A tab with nothing in it teaches people the app is empty**, so Feed is hidden rather than
  seeded-with-filler or left up. It still renders at `/?tab=feed`; restoring it is one entry in
  `lib/home-tab.ts`. The seeding plan is `features/community-feed-seeding.md`.
- **The marketplace was a small basket icon on another tab's heading.** Two tabs now, because
  "who is near me" and "what can I buy" are different questions. One component
  (`components/shop/Marketplace.tsx`) serves both `/shop` and the tab so they cannot drift.
- **The default view of Events is the calendar** — it needs no input and costs no model call, so
  it is what someone who has said nothing should land on. The ranked feed is opt-in.
- **Hiding a member is curation, not moderation** (`lib/hidden-members.ts`). Hidden ≠ deleted:
  reachable by direct id, still signed in, still runs its own portal. Filtered in SQL rather than
  after the fetch, or hidden rows eat slots out of the `limit` and silently shrink the feed. Every
  source needs it — the connector's event list is a second source and would have let a hidden
  member back in through the side door.
- **"Has an image" must be ONE predicate.** `hasMemberImage()` sits next to `MEMBER_HERO_IMAGES`
  and is the same list `MemberCard` renders, because two copies fail silently in both directions:
  a directory that hides a business whose photo would have rendered, or one that promises a photo
  and draws a gradient. (Same shape as the image-hosts bug.)
- **A hidden control is not access control.** The nav `+` went vendor-only, and `/share` had to
  bounce server-side too — the URL is typeable, bookmarkable and in history.
- **`/share` is two doors wearing one URL.** Bare = the business composer (vendors). Tagged
  (`?business=`, `?event=`) = the memories flow behind "Been here? Post a photo" / "Tag" / "Post
  your vibe", open to any signed-in person: those walls exist to gather what the CROWD posted, and
  vendor-gating them would quietly empty the feature they feed. Found only by grepping who links
  to `/share` — the blanket bounce would have turned four visible CTAs into dead ends.
- **`useMyMemberId` is the first client-side "am I a vendor" signal in the app.** Keyed on the
  endpoint so the whole app shares one answer per session, gated on `isSignedIn` because most
  visitors are signed out and that endpoint 401s (which the default SWR fetcher throws on). UI
  gated on it stays hidden while loading — an appearing control is a smaller lie than a
  disappearing one.

## Recent Decisions
- **Semantic personalisation shipped — the "One feed, six people" prototype is finally wired in (2026-08-11, migration `20260811140000` applied + registered; NOT yet deployed).** The embedding half had existed since the Aug 2 prototype and was **dead code**: `lib/reco/embed.ts` was complete, `lib/reco/profile.ts` defined a whole `ShopperProfile`, `rank.ts` had accepted a `profileVector` since day one — and the only caller was `scripts/try-personas.ts`, reading a 23MB `data/embeddings.json` on one laptop. Production ranked on **literal word overlap** (`keywordScore`) and stored **nothing** about any shopper. Now: events carry `embedding` (backfilled 697 — **323 replayed free from the prototype file**, 374 embedded for $0.0009), shoppers get an editable `shopper_taste` row, and the feed blends the two.
  - **Similarity is computed in SQL, never in Node.** Selecting 1200 × 1536 floats to produce 1200 numbers is ~20MB of JSON per request; `event_similarity()` returns `(id, sim)` and the vectors never leave Postgres. `rankEvents` gained a `similarities` option alongside `vectors` so the offline persona script and production land on identical maths.
  - **NO vector index, deliberately.** An ANN top-K answers the wrong question: similarity is 1 of 6 weighted signals, so the ranker needs a score for EVERY candidate or proximity gets silently overridden by a pre-truncated list. Full scan on ~700 rows is milliseconds. Revisit at ~50k with a pre-filter, not a top-K.
  - **The profile is a paragraph the person can read, edit and delete — never an embedded transcript.** The chat tuner (`/api/shopper/taste/chat`, tool-loop modelled on the vendor agent tuner) writes the SAME `about` text and chips shown on `/shopper`. A raw accumulating transcript drifts, over-weights whatever was said last, and cannot be corrected by the person it describes.
  - **Works signed-out**, keyed on a browser-minted `device:<uuid>` (`lib/taste-id.ts`); a Clerk id always wins and a client-supplied id is ignored once signed in, so nobody can name a stranger's row. Most shoppers never sign in — gating this on an account would ship it to almost nobody.
  - **A search stays one-off unless they tap "Remember this".** Folding every query in automatically is how "tickets for my mum's birthday" shapes someone's feed forever. When a saved profile IS used the feed says so out loud, with a link to change or delete it.
  - **A typed sentence beats the saved profile but does not erase it** (`blend`, 0.7/0.3, re-normalised — an un-normalised query silently compresses every score toward zero).
  - **Cost rule held:** embedding happens once per new event at ingest (`embedNew()` in the nightly sweep, beside `labelNew()`) and once per *changed* profile (`embedded_text` comparison makes an unchanged save free). The only per-request model calls are the persona parse and one small sentence embedding, run concurrently.
  - **Verified:** `npx tsx scripts/taste-smoke.mts` — 25 checks against the real DB (identity spoofing refused, model-mismatch guard, emptying clears the vector, delete removes the row, every live event scored). Plus HTTP probes showing the feed change from generic to family/free events after a save, and a browser walk of the tuner, the chat, and "Remember this". Anon key **refused** both read and write on `shopper_taste` while `vendor_events` stayed readable. **Backfill:** `npx tsx scripts/embed-stored-events.ts --from-file`.

Nothing asked it from the vendor's side, so a vendor who ACCEPTED an invite watched the event vanish: "My events" lists only what they HOST, and the event lived on the organizer's screen and the public page. They had signed up for something with nowhere to see it. Deliberately **NOT plan-gated** — hosting is an Organizer capability but *being invited* is Free (`networkReceive`), so gating it would hide the answer from exactly the vendors who need it. Self-hides when empty, sorts soonest-first (an unreadable date sorts LAST, never squatting at the top), drops finished events on a stated end date, and shows the **role** they agreed to turn up as — the one thing not on the public page. Verified against real data: accepted lineups found from the vendor side, pending invites excluded, another member's lineup not returned.
- **Collab + lineup invites were PUSH-ONLY; now push + email (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED).** All three invite paths (`api/vendor/invites` POST, `.../invites/[id]` accept, `api/vendor/events/[eventId]/lineup` bulk-invite) called `notifyMemberSafe` from `lib/push.ts` instead of `notifyMemberUserSafe` from `lib/notify.ts` — so a vendor without the iOS app registered got **nothing at all** when an organizer added them to a lineup. Since most vendors are on the web, most lineup invites were silent. This is precisely the **Notifications** convention in Key Conventions ("notify = push + email, ALWAYS, via `lib/notify.ts`") not being followed at the call site. **Audit any new `notifyMemberSafe` call for the same thing** — the push-only primitive is right only when an email would be noise.

Invites land in `/vendor/messages` → **Collaborations**, which is one of the two primary nav tabs, and `/vendor/network` redirects there.
- **Two real bugs found in a self-audit and fixed (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED).** Both were the same shape as bugs this repo has been bitten by before, which is why the audit was worth doing before moving on.
  1. **A client could pick its way out of paying postage.** `create-payment-intent` only computed `isPod` for baskets the *client* had already called a delivery, so a crafted request with `fulfillmentType: 'pickup'` over print-on-demand items skipped the Printify postage quote entirely and created a pickup order — leaving the vendor owing a customer an item Printify ships, with the postage never collected. **A POD basket now forces `delivery` server-side, computed before the fulfillment choice.** Same rule as prices: the basket decides, never the caller.
  2. **Digital delivery silently dropped de-listed products.** `deliverDigitalItems` used `getProductsByMember`, which filters `active = true` — so a vendor taking a product off sale between payment and the webhook left the buyer with nothing AND no error, because the lookup simply missed. Now uses `getAllProductsByMember`: **they paid, so whether it's still listed is irrelevant.** Regression test added.
  - Also corrected: the Square scope guidance listed only `APPOINTMENTS_*`, but availability is read from the **catalog** (`ITEMS_READ`) and Square requires a customer on every booking (`CUSTOMERS_WRITE`). Listing two of the four would have sent every vendor back for a second token.
- **Square Appointments shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810180000`. ⚠️ Live API UNVERIFIED — but sandbox-verifiable.** `lib/square-appointments.ts` + `app/api/vendor/square-appointments` + public `app/api/bookings/availability` + `components/vendor/SquareBookingsCard.tsx`.
  - **It confirms AGAINST `booking_requests`; there is no second booking model.** A Square vendor's slots are real, so picking one **books it outright and the row lands `confirmed`** — asking the owner to re-approve a time Square already reserved would be theatre. Everyone else keeps the free-text request, and neither the customer form nor the vendor inbox needs to know which kind it is.
  - **`square_env` (production | sandbox) is an EXPLICIT column, never sniffed from the token** — the two token types aren't reliably distinguishable, and guessing points real customer bookings at a test account or vice versa. It is also what makes this integration verifiable before a real business is exposed to it.
  - **The predictable failure is scopes, and it needs FOUR:** `APPOINTMENTS_READ`, `APPOINTMENTS_WRITE`, **`ITEMS_READ`** (availability is read from the catalog — service variations) and **`CUSTOMERS_WRITE`** (Square requires a customer on every booking). A Square link made for catalog/orders (Composio) has none of them, so a vendor who "already connected Square" lands on `INSUFFICIENT_SCOPES`. The connect card says so *before* they try, and the error handler names it explicitly.
  - **Square schedules a service VARIATION** (a 45-min cut vs a 90-min colour), not a service — so a variation id is what availability search and booking creation both name. `service_duration` arrives in **milliseconds**.
  - **Every Square failure falls back to a plain request** rather than erroring: a slot taken in the meantime, wrong scopes, or an outage still lets the customer ask, which is what every other business offers anyway. An availability lookup that throws returns `realCalendar: false`, so a Square outage can never take the Book button down.
  - **Cancelling or declining releases the Square slot** (`releaseSquareSlot`), or the business holds a gap for someone who isn't coming. Disconnecting deliberately does NOT cancel existing bookings — those are real appointments.
  - Credentials join Printify's in **`vendor_secrets`** (service-role only). Verified with a real anon client that the token can't be read.
  - **Verified (what could be):** `npx tsx scripts/square-smoke.mts` — 12 checks on credential isolation, env round-trip, the DB rejecting an unknown environment, and booked-vs-requested record shape; plus HTTP probes showing no-Square degrades to `realCalendar:false` and a normal request still succeeding. **Unverified: locations, services, availability, customer and booking creation.**
- **Bookings: request-to-book shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810170000`.** `booking_requests` + `lib/bookings.ts` + `app/api/bookings` (public) + `app/api/vendor/bookings` + `components/booking/BookingRequest.tsx` + `/vendor/bookings`.
  - **What it replaced:** the **Book button on every live business profile opened a PROTOTYPE** — `ActionBar` imported `BookFlow` from `app/prototype/host/page.tsx`, which read hardcoded `VENUES` from `lib/prototype-data.ts`, made **zero API calls**, stored nothing and notified nobody. A customer could "book" and no one would ever know. The fake slot fixture in `ActionBar` is deleted.
  - **It is deliberately NOT a calendar.** No availability, no slots, no double-booking logic — we don't have the business's diary, and inventing availability is a worse lie than asking. It models the conversation that actually happens: "could I come Thursday around 2?" → "yes" / "not then, how about Friday?".
  - **Time is FREE TEXT** ("afternoon", "after 5") — a strict picker makes people lie to the form. The date is sortable ISO.
  - **Confirming may name a DIFFERENT time from the one asked**, because that's the commonest real reply; `requested_*` is never overwritten, so what was asked stays legible next to what was agreed.
  - **No account needed** (guest id derived from the email, same as tickets), and the customer is emailed on confirm/decline — a request answered into silence is the same as one never answered.
  - **Open requests sort first regardless of date** — an unanswered request is the only thing needing action, and burying it under next month's confirmed bookings is how people get ignored.
  - **Verified:** `npx tsx scripts/bookings-smoke.mts` (19 checks — cross-business confirm refused, cross-customer cancel refused, moved-time recording, ordering) plus HTTP probes: validation messages, auth on the vendor inbox, and a real guest request persisting to the DB.
- **Printify print-on-demand shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810160000`. ⚠️ UNVERIFIED AGAINST THE LIVE API.** Every other commerce feature was exercised against its real upstream before shipping; this one could not be, because no vendor has connected a Printify account. Response shapes are read defensively so drift degrades to "no products" rather than a crash, but **the first real connect is the test.** `lib/printify.ts` (REST, no SDK) + `lib/printify-commerce.ts` + `app/api/vendor/printify` + `components/vendor/PrintifyCard.tsx`.
  - **Credentials live in a NEW `vendor_secrets` table, service-role ONLY — never put them in `vendor_settings`, which grants SELECT to `anon`.** A token that can spend a vendor's money and reprint their catalog must not sit in an anon-readable table. Verified for real with the anon key: reads and writes are both refused, while `vendor_settings` is readable — which is the whole argument for the split. The token is **never returned by any endpoint**, not even masked.
  - **Printify uses personal access tokens, not OAuth** (unlike Shopify/Square via Composio), so the vendor pastes one in. `listShops` IS the validity check — storing a token we've never used is how Composio ended up showing "Connected" over an abandoned consent screen.
  - **One marketplace row per VARIANT, not per product.** Printify prices and ships a variant (a size, a colour), so a row naming only the product would have no price to charge and nothing specific to print. Imported as **drafts** (`active: false`) into the same approval queue as an AI-scanned menu; a re-sync updates price/images but **never flips `active` back on**, so a vendor's takedown sticks.
  - **Postage is quoted from Printify BEFORE payment** and, crucially, **the fee routes to the VENDOR** — Printify bills *them* for production and postage, so keeping it would make them pay twice. The rule across all three providers is now simply *whoever pays the carrier keeps the fee*: uber → platform, self → vendor, printify → vendor. Platform's 5% is on items only, always.
  - **A POD basket overrides the vendor's delivery mode**, resolved per basket (one shop can sell both): it's printed and posted, so pickup and vendor-driving are impossible regardless of what they set. The picker hides the pickup/delivery toggle entirely.
  - Order push is **idempotent via `external_id` = our order number**, so a webhook retry is refused by Printify as a duplicate rather than printing a second shirt. Best-effort: the buyer has paid, so a Printify outage logs rather than throwing.
  - **Disconnecting leaves imported products alone** — they may have sold, and deleting a catalog as a side effect of unlinking is a destructive surprise.
  - **Verified (what could be):** `npx tsx scripts/printify-smoke.mts` — 15 checks on credential isolation, the no-creds no-op, address mapping (one-word and empty names still produce both name fields, since Printify rejects an empty last name and the buyer has already paid), and the fee split. **Unverified: shops, products, shipping and order calls.**
- **Digital delivery shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810150000`.** The other half of `kind='digital'`: `products.digital_file_*` + a `digital_grants` table + `lib/digital.ts` / `lib/digital-deliver.ts` + `GET /download/[token]`. `digital` is now offered in the vendor product picker.
  - **The bucket is PRIVATE (`digital-goods`), unlike `marketplace-media`.** A public bucket makes the object URL the product: paste it anywhere and the paywall is gone permanently. The buyer never sees a storage URL — every redemption mints a **fresh signed URL, 5-minute TTL**, so a forwarded link dies while the grant keeps working. **Signing requires `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`** (the anon key cannot); `digitalConfigured()` gates the upload route so a vendor can't upload something no buyer could ever fetch.
  - **The grant token is the credential**, same rule as a ticket: a guest buyer has no account, so 128 random bits in an emailed link is the access control. `/download/[token]` is deliberately unauthenticated and **302s** (never 301 — a cached permanent redirect to an expired signed URL would fail forever).
  - **Delivery runs on EVERY paid order, not just `fulfillment_type='digital'`.** A mixed basket is classed physical (the sandwich must change hands) but the ebook in it still has to arrive — gating on the order's type would silently swallow every download bought alongside a physical item. Idempotent on the payment intent, so the browser confirm and the webhook can't double-send.
  - **No download cap by default** (`max_downloads` NULL = unlimited): re-downloading on a new phone two years later is the normal case, not abuse. A cap is the vendor's choice. Downloads are counted **after** the URL is minted, so a signing failure never burns one.
  - **A digital product cannot be saved without a file** — blocked in `ProductsManager`, because the alternative is discovering it after the buyer has paid. Filenames are randomised in storage but the buyer's download keeps the vendor's original name.
  - **Verified end to end against real storage:** `npx tsx scripts/digital-smoke.mts` — 20 checks including the unsigned object returning **400**, the signed URL returning the **actual paid bytes**, two redemptions minting different URLs, cap/expiry refusals, and a download bought alongside bread still being delivered.
- **Product kinds shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810140000`.** `products.kind` = `good | service | digital | ticket`, plus `orders.fulfillment_type` gaining `digital` and `service`. This fixes a live lie: a $150 consultation (the seed data sells one) took real money and then told the buyer to **"Pick up"** and the vendor to **"Mark Ready"**, because nothing in the schema told a consultation from a loaf of bread. Vocabulary lives in **`lib/product-kind.ts`** — import it, never re-derive.
  - **`kindOf()` fails SAFE to `good`.** Every pre-migration row, every AI-scanned menu item and every POS import arrives without a kind; reading those as non-physical would skip a handover the customer is standing there waiting for.
  - **A MIXED basket is PHYSICAL** (`basketFulfillment`). If one item has to change hands the order still needs a pickup or delivery; the download rides along either way, the sandwich doesn't. Treating it as digital strands the physical item with nobody scheduled to hand it over.
  - **Fulfillment is derived server-side from the catalog**, in `create-payment-intent` — a client claiming `digital` for a sandwich must not talk its way out of a handover, the same rule as prices.
  - A service is **`completed`**, never `collected` — you don't collect an hour of someone's time. A digital order is `delivered` **the moment payment lands**: there is no vendor action, so it must never sit in a queue waiting for one.
  - `digital` was hidden from the vendor picker until delivery existed; it is **now live** (see Digital delivery below).
  - **Verified:** `npx tsx scripts/product-kind-smoke.mts` (21 checks, heavy on the fail-safe and mixed-basket cases) plus a live run: goods offered pickup/delivery, a service and a download offered neither, and ebook+sourdough correctly stayed physical. A $90 tarot reading now reads **"Booked with the business"** in a browser instead of "Pick up".
- **Self-delivery shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED), migration `20260810130000`.** A vendor can now deliver their own orders — which matters because **Uber is blocked on account activation, so before this NOBODY could offer delivery at all**. `vendor_settings.delivery_mode` (`none` | `self` | `uber`) is the new source of truth; `uber_direct_enabled` stays and is written in step for legacy readers. **Read it through `effectiveDeliveryMode()`, never raw** — a stored `uber` collapses to `none` without platform credentials, and self-delivery deliberately has no such dependency.
  - **THE MONEY IS THE WHOLE POINT.** `uber` → the platform pays the courier, so the fee is added to `application_fee_amount` and stays with us. `self` → the vendor IS the courier, so the fee is **deliberately NOT added** and flows through to them (`items + fee` charged, `5% of items` kept). Adding it in both branches would silently skim every self-delivering vendor; the branch in `create-payment-intent` carries a comment saying so. Platform still never taxes the delivery fee in either mode.
  - **Coverage is POSTAL CODES, not a radius** — a radius needs the buyer's address geocoded on every checkout (a billed Google request per attempt, against the `lib/places.ts` spending rules) and is fuzzy exactly where it matters. **Empty/NULL `self_delivery_zips` means ANYWHERE**, the zero-typing default; reading it as "nowhere" would make switching delivery on do nothing.
  - Rules live in `lib/fulfillment.ts` (`selfDeliveryRules` / `quoteSelfDelivery`, pure + free): flat fee, optional free-over threshold, optional minimum order. **The minimum is reported before the area** — it's the failure the buyer can actually fix. Blank fields are `null` ("no such rule"), never 0 — a 0 free-over threshold would make every order free.
  - `orders.delivery_provider` records which kind it was. `dispatched` means "courier has it" for uber and "the vendor is driving it over" for self, so the orders UI relabels it **Out for delivery** and shows a plain status button instead of a dispatch call. `/api/uber/dispatch` **refuses a self order** (400) — that endpoint spends money, so the guard is server-side, not just UI.
  - Buyer sees the vendor's terms (fee, free-over, minimum, ZIPs, "when you deliver" note) **before typing an address**, since the fee is knowable from the basket alone. Quoted by `/api/checkout/self-delivery`, which re-prices the basket from the vendor's ACTIVE catalog — a client that could name its own subtotal could talk itself past a minimum or into free delivery.
  - **Verified:** `npx tsx scripts/self-delivery-smoke.mts` (26 checks — rules, precedence, and the fee split arithmetic mirroring create-payment-intent) plus a live run through the local server against a seeded vendor: below-minimum, covered ZIP, free-over, out-of-area, and a fabricated line item all behaved. Checkout was driven in a browser. **NOT verified: no real card has been charged**, so the payout split is proven in arithmetic and code, not in a Stripe balance.
- **Ticketing + QR check-in shipped (2026-08-10, DEPLOYED).** Full detail in the Ticketing bullet under Architecture. - **`features/community-feed-seeding.md` (spec, 2026-08-13) — how the dead Feed tab gets seeded.** Events and the directory are alive because they were seeded from reality; the feed never got its version, so it asks users to go first. The governing constraint is that **whatever is in the feed teaches people what the feed is for** — so the seed must look like the posts we want, not like a feed of records. Consequences worth knowing before touching it: the content axis is **stakes vs logistics** (an eviction, not a Muni delay — logistics is also commodity, so it costs permanent scraper maintenance and buys no distinctiveness); auto content is **filler that retreats**, never taking the top slot when a human post exists and rendering less as real posts arrive; posts are signed by visible house accounts (`WhatsLocal`, `WhatsLocal <Neighborhood>`) and **never by a person as their own experience** — a generated "best burrito in the city" is a fabricated endorsement naming a real business; the **house voice is hand-written, ~40 posts dripped, NOT nightly-generated** (the one place automation makes the product worse); ranking is **stakes × proximity decay**, not radius tiers, or a citywide landmark story gets buried under a permit on your street. Auto items live in **their own table, never `posts`**, so a retention sweep cannot reach a user's post. **Phase 1 needs no scrapers at all** (directory activity + house voice) and is the right thing to ship and live with first. Contains a 24-example approved corpus that doubles as the generation few-shot and the review bar.
