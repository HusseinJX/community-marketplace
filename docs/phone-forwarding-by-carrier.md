# Send your missed calls to your AI agent

Keep your number. Keep answering it yourself. The only thing that changes is
what happens to the calls you *don't* pick up — instead of dropping into
voicemail, they go to your business's AI agent, which answers as you, knows your
prices and hours, and takes a message that lands in your inbox.

Nothing to install. No new number to print. You set one code on your phone, and
you can undo it in five seconds.

**Forward your calls to: `+1 562-257-3224`**

---

## The one thing that goes wrong

Your phone company already has a rule for unanswered calls: *send them to
voicemail after about 15–20 seconds.* When you add call forwarding, you're
adding a second rule — and **whichever fires first wins.**

Set your forwarding to 20 seconds and voicemail will usually grab the call at
~16 seconds. Your forwarding is switched on, correctly configured, and never
gets a turn. Your phone will even confirm it's "Enabled", because it is.

**So set the forwarding timer LOW — start at 5 seconds.**

And a wrinkle worth knowing: **the number you set is a floor, not a promise.**
Carriers round it up. On Boost, a 5-second setting actually forwarded at 10–11
seconds — which is a comfortable number of rings for you to answer first, and
still safely ahead of voicemail. If you set 10, you may land at 15+ and lose the
race again. **Start low; you can always test and raise it.**

## Boost Mobile — ✅ tested and confirmed working

These are the exact codes we tested end to end. Dial each one like a phone
number and press call; you'll get a confirmation message.

| What | Code |
|---|---|
| Forward when you don't answer | `**61*+15622573224*11*5#` |
| Forward when you're on another call | `**67*+15622573224#` |
| Forward when your phone is off / no signal | `**62*+15622573224#` |
| **Check what's set** | `*#61#` |
| **Turn it off** | `##61#` (also `##67#`, `##62#`) |

The first one is the important one — that's "I didn't pick up." The other two
close the gaps where a caller would otherwise hit voicemail.

The `*11*5` on the end means *voice calls, 5 seconds*. Keep the `*11*`; change
only the `5` if you want to ring longer.

**Measured on Boost:** voicemail answers at ~16 seconds; a 5-second setting
forwards at 10–11 seconds. Your phone rings for about three rings first.

## Other carriers

Boost is the only one we've confirmed ourselves. The codes below are the
industry-standard ones and should work, but **check with `*#61#` and place a
test call before relying on them.**

### T-Mobile, AT&T, Mint, Cricket, Metro, Google Fi, and most others

Same codes as Boost above. These are the standard GSM codes and work on the
large majority of US carriers.

### Verizon

Verizon uses an older, different scheme, and **you cannot set the timer** — you
get the carrier's default ring time.

| What | Code |
|---|---|
| Forward when busy or unanswered | `*71` then the number, e.g. `*715622573224` |
| Forward every call (phone never rings) | `*72` then the number |
| **Turn it off** | `*73` |

Because the timer isn't adjustable on Verizon, the voicemail race above is out of
your hands. If test calls keep landing in voicemail, use "forward every call"
during the hours you can't answer, or call Verizon and ask them to shorten your
"no answer transfer" timer.

### Landline, VoIP, or a business phone system

RingCentral, Grasshopper, Ooma, Vonage, Google Voice, or a desk-phone system:
there are no star codes to dial — set it in the provider's admin dashboard.
Look for **Call Forwarding**, **Call Handling** or **Rules**, and forward
*unanswered* calls to `+1 562-257-3224`. These systems usually let you set the
ring time in seconds, which makes the voicemail race easy to win.

## Check it worked

1. Dial `*#61#`. It should say **Voice Call Forwarding — When Unanswered —
   Forwards to +15622573224 — Enabled.**
2. Have someone call you from another phone and **let it ring out.**
3. It should be answered by your agent, not your voicemail.

If you reach voicemail instead, your timer is losing the race — lower it and try
again. If your phone rings forever and nothing answers, the person calling you is
probably calling *from* the forwarding number; try a different phone.

## Turning it off

`##61#` (plus `##67#` and `##62#` if you set those), or `*73` on Verizon. Takes
effect immediately. `##002#` clears every kind of forwarding at once if you want
a clean slate.

## What to expect

- **Your phone still rings first.** You always get the chance to answer.
- **Callers never see our number.** They dial yours; the hand-off is invisible.
- **The agent says it's an AI**, up front, on every call.
- **Calls are not recorded.** You get a written transcript and any message the
  caller left, in your Messages inbox.
- **Missed-call messages arrive as a notification and an email**, so you can call
  the person back.

## If some calls still reach voicemail

Set all three codes, not just the first — a caller who rings while you're already
on a call hits *busy*, which is a different rule (`**67*`), and one who calls
while your phone is off hits *unreachable* (`**62*`). With only the "no answer"
rule set, those two still go to voicemail.
